# Fuji - Target User, In Detail

---

## The one-line version

Fuji is for a typical ACG lover who prefers low-pressure interaction, and wants a small expressive companion for tiny everyday decisions and gentle company, without opening a phone.

This is a beachhead user, not a claim that Fuji is only for anime fans. The first product should earn love from people who already treat cute characters, plushies, badges, and themed accessories as part of their identity. General-purpose assistant features can broaden the audience later.

---

## Primary persona

### Lin (representative, not a statistical claim)

| Attribute            | Description                                                                                                                                                         |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Age / life stage     | 18-30; university student, early-career worker, or graduate student                                                                                                 |
| Living pattern       | Urban or university environment; regularly moves between home, campus/work, shops, cafes, and transit                                                               |
| Interests            | Anime, games, illustration, cosplay, character goods, themed cafes, online fandom, and sharing finds with a small trusted circle                                    |
| Device habits        | Smartphone-first; comfortable with messaging and selective voice use, but dislikes speaking loudly in public or spending energy navigating a screen for a tiny task |
| Spending pattern     | Will spend on character goods and small accessories when the object feels distinctive, durable, and emotionally worthwhile                                          |
| Technical confidence | Can pair devices and enable a phone hotspot, but does not want to debug firmware, configure APIs, or manage a complicated smart-home setup                          |
| Social context       | Often alone or with one or two trusted friends; enjoys quiet personal rituals and may be hesitant to perform for a larger group                                     |
| Current workaround   | Phone search, map app, saved notes, messaging a friend, or a keychain/plushie that provides emotional value but no active help                                      |

### A representative moment

Lin is leaving class alone and feels hungry but has little energy to compare options. Lin says, "Fuji, find me something nearby," gives one constraint when asked (for example, "not spicy and under 50 RMB"), and listens to two or three options. Fuji makes a playful recommendation, waits for confirmation, opens navigation on the phone, and gives short spoken turns while Lin walks. Later, with one close friend, the same flow can break a small deadlock without turning Lin into the group's host. In either case, Lin can tap Fuji's head for reassurance; Fuji responds with a small expression or sound rather than demanding a conversation.

The important value is a complete low-pressure moment: Fuji reduces friction and adds a little warmth, whether Lin is alone or with trusted people. A restaurant search by itself is not enough, and cuteness by itself is not enough.

### Needs and tensions

| What Lin needs                            | What makes it difficult today                                                                      | Product implication                                                                                                   |
| ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A fast answer to a low-stakes decision    | Too many options, low mental energy, and the effort of stopping to search or message someone       | Use short voice turns, a small number of options, and a visible/voice confirmation step                               |
| A companion that feels personal           | Most assistants are functional but emotionally flat; plushies are warm but inert                   | Give Fuji a consistent character, expressive face, touch response, and memory that is transparent and editable        |
| Help while moving                         | Looking down at a phone is inconvenient and sometimes unsafe                                       | Keep interaction hands-free; use the phone only as a quiet gateway for maps, network, and permissions                 |
| A wearable object that survives real life | A shoulder-mounted object can fall, snag clothing, attract unwanted attention, or run out of power | Engineer magnet retention, one-handed removal, privacy feedback, weather tolerance, and a predictable battery routine |
| Trustworthy recommendations               | Generic recommendations ignore taste, budget, distance, or the preferences of a small group        | Ask only the missing constraint, explain why an option fits, and let the user correct the preference                  |

### Behaviours that indicate a good fit

The persona is a strong fit when the user:

- already follows ACG culture or carries a character object, even if they prefer subtle or private forms of enthusiasm;
- has at least one recurring daily micro-decision that can be solved in under two minutes (food, route, translation, reminder, or "pick one"), alone or with a trusted person;
- uses a phone hotspot or is willing to pair Fuji with a phone for network access;
- values an object responding with warmth and personality, not only an answer, but does not want constant attention;
- is willing to give lightweight feedback ("too far," "not spicy," "remember this") so recommendations improve;
- accepts that Fuji is a focused companion with a few excellent flows, rather than a full phone replacement.

### Behaviours that indicate a poor fit

The user is probably not in the first target if they want a silent ornament only, require completely offline operation, expect medical/safety-critical decisions, refuse any phone pairing, or want a general assistant with broad app automation on day one.

## Jobs to be done (functional / emotional / social)

### Core job statement

When I have a small decision, a low-energy moment, or a need for gentle company, I want to speak naturally to a cute companion and get a concise, trustworthy next step, so that I can keep moving without taking on another demanding interaction.

### Functional jobs

1. **Decide what to eat.** Combine location, opening status, distance, budget, dietary constraints, and known preferences to return a short list or a single playful pick.
2. **Start and narrate a route.** With explicit confirmation, hand the destination to the phone's map app and give brief turn or progress prompts through the device.
3. **Handle small language gaps.** Translate a short spoken phrase or sign and repeat it at a useful pace; make it clear when the result is uncertain.
4. **Remember a lightweight commitment.** Create, read, and remind about a simple event or task, with the time and wording repeated for confirmation.
5. **Respond to the user's presence.** React to voice, touch, and orientation with expressions or small actions that make the object feel alive without consuming attention.
6. **Recover gracefully.** When Wi-Fi, permissions, speech recognition, or a map handoff fails, explain the limitation and offer a usable fallback rather than pretending success.

### Emotional jobs

- Make an ordinary day feel lighter and a bad mood feel acknowledged without making a serious mental-health claim.
- Feel accompanied in transit, while waiting, or when doing a repetitive task.
- Express taste and identity through an object that is "mine," not a generic black gadget.
- Feel delight from a response that is surprising in a small, safe, character-consistent way.
- Feel in control: Fuji should be affectionate, never clingy, manipulative, or noisy when the user wants quiet.

### Social jobs

- Make a small decision with one or two trusted people without becoming the organiser or spokesperson.
- Give close friends a reason to talk, laugh, or ask about the object without forcing a performance.
- Help the wearer contribute to a group plan while their hands are occupied.
- Signal shared fandom or taste in a way that can be private, subtle, or legible to the user's community.

### Desired outcomes and first-release measures

| Outcome                                          | Early measure                                                                                                                                |
| ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The user can finish a meal-decision flow quickly | Median time from wake word to confirmed choice; target under 90 seconds in a quiet pilot                                                     |
| Fuji does not demand attention                   | Fewer than one unsolicited interaction per hour outside an active request; user-rated interruption score                                     |
| Recommendations feel relevant                    | At least 70% of pilot choices rated "would consider" and a clear reason for each suggestion                                                  |
| The device is remembered and used                | At least 4 active days per week after week two; repeat use of two or more flows                                                              |
| The personality creates attachment               | Users can recall Fuji's character and report at least one moment of comfort or delight, without confusing it for a human or a safety service |
| The system is trusted                            | Users understand when Fuji is listening, when an external action is pending, and when a request failed                                       |

## Willingness to pay

### What the user is actually paying for

The purchase is a combination of a character object, a wearable accessory, and a small voice utility. Lin is unlikely to pay a premium for "AI" as an abstract capability; they may pay for a design they want to own, reliable interactions, and a companion that becomes part of a routine.

### Initial pricing hypothesis

Use a modest hardware price appropriate for a student or early-career buyer, with no mandatory subscription for the core experience. A reasonable validation range is **RMB 199-399** for the device and magnetic mount, depending on enclosure, display, battery, and audio quality. Treat this as a test range, not a final price.

Paid add-ons can come later (character skins, accessories, or optional cloud features), but essential wake, expression, touch, and the core food/route flows should not be paywalled. A recurring fee would be a major adoption barrier for a novelty companion unless it clearly covers a costly service and is explained before purchase.

### Evidence to collect before committing

- Which existing character goods Lin buys, at what price, and how often;
- whether the user prefers a physical prototype, a phone-only simulation, or both;
- whether reliable navigation and reminders change purchase intent more than extra character content;
- acceptable charging frequency and tolerance for a phone hotspot;
- the price at which the product feels like a fun accessory versus an unjustified gadget.

## Adoption barriers (be honest)

| Barrier                                    | Why it matters                                                                                    | Design response / validation                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "It is cute, but I will stop using it."    | Novelty products are easy to admire and abandon                                                   | Anchor the character in two repeatable utilities; measure week-two and week-four retention                                                                    |
| Phone hotspot and pairing friction         | Network setup can defeat the promise of instant conversation                                      | Provide a one-time pairing flow, clear connection state, and a graceful offline personality mode                                                              |
| Battery anxiety                            | A wearable that dies during a day is worse than a keychain                                        | Publish a realistic battery target, low-power idle behavior, battery warning, and simple charging dock/cable                                                  |
| Public embarrassment or unwanted attention | Voice interaction and cute sounds may feel too conspicuous                                        | Offer quiet mode, adjustable volume, haptic/face-only acknowledgements, and a discrete wake gesture                                                           |
| Privacy and accidental listening           | A shoulder companion is physically close to private conversations                                 | Visible listening indicator, hardware mute, local wake-word explanation, recording retention controls, and explicit confirmation before external actions      |
| Recommendation quality                     | A wrong restaurant or route quickly breaks trust                                                  | Ask for missing constraints, show source/time for external data where possible, and never claim a booking or navigation handoff happened without confirmation |
| Physical safety and durability             | Magnets, drops, rain, clothing, and movement create real failure modes                            | Prototype mount strength, skin/clothing compatibility, rounded enclosure, splash tolerance, and a tether option                                               |
| Character mismatch                         | A voice or joke that feels childish, intrusive, or culturally awkward can repel the intended user | Co-design personality scripts with target users; define boundaries and a quiet/serious response mode                                                          |
| App integration limits                     | Maps, reminders, and translation permissions vary by phone and region                             | Start with one supported phone pathway and document fallbacks; do not promise universal automation                                                            |
| Accessibility and speech recognition       | Accents, noise, and hearing/vision differences can block the main interaction                     | Add push-to-talk/touch fallback, captions or phone transcript, adjustable speech rate, and noise tests                                                        |

### Non-negotiable trust boundaries

Fuji must not present itself as a therapist, emergency service, medical adviser, or authority on high-stakes decisions. It should encourage the user to contact a person or appropriate service when a request exceeds its scope. The product can support mood-lifting and companionship, but it must not imply human consciousness or guarantee privacy beyond the actual system behavior.

## Anti-persona (who we are NOT building for)

1. **The fully offline buyer.** They require all useful voice understanding and recommendations to work without a phone or network. Fuji's first concept depends on a phone hotspot and external services for maps and search.
2. **The power automator.** They want a broad, programmable smart-home or app-control hub with arbitrary integrations. That would pull the product toward a different architecture and support burden.
3. **The purely silent utility buyer.** They want a neutral tool with no character, expression, or emotional response. Fuji's warmth is deliberately part of the product, even though it must be controllable and discreet.
4. **The safety-critical user.** They need guaranteed medical, navigation, security, or emergency outcomes. Fuji can assist with low-stakes everyday coordination but is not a dependable safety device.
5. **The child as an unsupervised account holder.** Children may enjoy Fuji, but the first release should not assume independent consent, data management, or unrestricted voice access. A child-focused version would require a separate privacy and guardian design.
6. **The user who expects a human substitute.** Fuji can acknowledge feelings and offer gentle interaction, but it should not encourage dependency, secrecy, or the belief that it can replace friends or professional help.

## Secondary personas (halo, not the target)

### 1. The commuter who wants hands-free micro-help

They may not identify with anime culture but like a small expressive object and need translation, reminders, or route prompts while carrying bags. They validate utility and quiet-mode design, but they should not dilute Fuji's character language in the first release.

### 2. The collector / maker

They care about enclosure variants, mounts, firmware, and customization. They can become an enthusiastic early tester and accessory buyer. They are helpful for finding hardware flaws, but they are not representative of the less technical everyday user Fuji must serve.

### 3. The friend-group facilitator

They naturally use Fuji as a neutral chooser for meals, activities, or group reminders. They help test social value, consent before recommendations, and whether one person can operate Fuji without making everyone else wait.

### 4. The gift buyer

They purchase Fuji for a fandom-oriented friend and judge it from the packaging, character clarity, and "first five minutes." They expose onboarding and explainability problems, but they do not necessarily become the long-term user.

## The one test question

**After using a rough Fuji prototype through an ordinary day, would you choose to keep it nearby next week for a specific recurring moment, and what exact moment would that be?**

Ask for the behaviour, not a compliment. A "yes" only counts when the user names a repeated context (for example, choosing dinner after class or using quiet company while commuting), can complete the flow without coaching, and still wants Fuji nearby after the novelty demonstration. Follow up with: "What would make you leave it at home?" to surface the real barrier.

### Assumptions to validate first

- The target user wants an expressive but controllable companion that works at home, while commuting, and discreetly in public.
- A phone hotspot is acceptable when setup is simple and the device clearly shows connection state.
- Food choice is frequent enough to become a habit, whether the user is alone or with a small trusted group, and broad enough to demonstrate recommendation quality.
- A small amount of memory improves usefulness without feeling invasive.
- Voice is useful when the user's hands or attention are occupied, while touch, text, and face-only feedback are essential fallback modes for low-energy or public moments.
